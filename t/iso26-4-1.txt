A laboratory has a capable team, yet results are delivered late.
Apply clause 4.1 to understand the conditions affecting service, rather than just asking people to work harder.
Turn that understanding into a decision, an owner and a way to track results.
Start with the laboratory purpose and direction: reliable service and controlled growth.
Identify relevant internal and external issues, and monitor and review information as circumstances change.
SWOT and PESTEL are optional tools; clause 4.1 does not prescribe either tool or a single template.
Bring operations, quality, resources and purchasing together to review delays, complaints, capacity and supplier performance.
Define the data period and involve the people doing the work, rather than relying on one opinion.
Classify issues, prioritize their effects and agree who will update the information and when.
PESTEL scans six external dimensions: political, economic, social, technological, environmental and legal.
Examples include supply costs, local demand, digital reporting and changes in applicable requirements.
Keep relevant issues and record their effects and evidence; do not fill every category merely to complete a diagram.
For this laboratory, a strength is experienced staff; a weakness is reliance on one evening-shift employee.
An opportunity is growing demand from nearby clinics; a threat is disruption to essential supplies.
Strengths and weaknesses are internal; opportunities and threats are external. Support each issue with evidence.
A broad statement such as staff shortage is not enough.
In this fictional example, 20% of evening-shift results were late during the month, while the roster shows reliance on one employee.
Investigate workload and delay causes; these observations alone do not prove that staffing is the sole cause.
Prioritize issues by their effect on service quality and continuity, the scale of change and urgency.
Start with shift coverage and supply reliability because both affect delivery commitments.
Keep the growth opportunity, but check capacity before accepting more work. This supports risk-and-opportunity planning under clause 6.1.
After checking delay causes, choose an appropriate action, such as qualifying backup staff and rebalancing evening work.
The operations manager owns a one-month plan; this example targets reducing late results from 20% to 10% or less.
Use a consistent measure: late results divided by all shift results. Also monitor accuracy so speed does not compromise quality.
