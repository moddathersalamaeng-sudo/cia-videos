At a commercial printer, printing is complete but inspection is waiting and the employee who knows the customer settings is absent.
A faster press alone will not resolve inspection capacity or missing knowledge and may increase work waiting for release.
Clause 7.1 concerns resources needed for actual results, beyond an inventory of equipment and purchases.
Determine and provide resources to establish, implement, maintain and continually improve the QMS.
The categories include people, infrastructure, process operating environment, monitoring and measuring resources, and organizational knowledge.
Following one print order shows how a missing resource can disrupt a result that depends on all of them.
Review order requirements, workload, available capacity and the capabilities and constraints of internal resources.
Then identify what is provided internally and what must be obtained externally, such as specialist maintenance or measurement services.
Connect decisions to objectives and risks and apply relevant external-provider controls; purchase alone does not establish suitability or availability.
Provide the people needed for effective QMS implementation and process operation and control, including inspection and planning.
Total staffing may be adequate while the inspector is unavailable when jobs finish, revealing a scheduling problem.
Providing people under 7.1 differs from demonstrating competence under 7.2, the subject of the next episode.
Infrastructure can include buildings, utilities, equipment, software, transport and ICT for on-site, remote or hybrid work.
Determine, provide and maintain what operations need: cutter maintenance, a reliable order system and connectivity for remote customer support.
The criterion is suitability for operations and conformity, rather than owning the latest equipment; address failures and constraints according to their impact.
Determine, provide and maintain the operating environment needed for conformity, considering relevant physical, social and psychological factors.
Review inspection lighting, paper-storage conditions and workload pressure; a culture that encourages hiding errors can also affect the operating environment.
Encourage honest reporting and address harmful pressure; determine conditions appropriate to the process rather than universal temperature or workplace rules.
When monitoring or measuring verifies product or service conformity, provide resources that yield valid and reliable results.
A dimensional measuring tool must suit the required measurement and acceptance limits, remain fit for purpose and have appropriate documented evidence.
Calibration alone cannot make an unsuitable tool appropriate; monitoring and measuring resources may also include methods other than instruments.
When results traceability is required or essential for confidence, calibrate, verify or both at set intervals or before use against standards traceable to international or national standards.
Where such standards do not exist, document the basis used; identify equipment status and protect it from adjustments, damage or deterioration that invalidate results.
If equipment is unfit, assess the validity of previous results and act as needed. Repair alone is insufficient; annual calibration is not universally prescribed.
In the 2026 edition, determine knowledge needed for operations and intended QMS results; retain, apply and share it to the extent necessary.
Retain approved customer settings and lessons learned, transfer their use to a colleague, and check application on an actual order; storing a file alone is insufficient.
As needs and trends change, review existing knowledge and how to acquire or access additions and updates through experience, methods, learning or digital systems.
Hypothetically, 20 batches require 12 inspection minutes each: 240 minutes in total.
Only 180 effective inspection minutes are available before delivery, leaving a 60-minute gap that faster printing cannot solve.
Consider rescheduling, an additional competent inspector or an agreed delivery change, without removing checks or weakening acceptance criteria.
If inspection capacity causes delay, assign suitable action, ownership, timing and resources, then assess delivery and error outcomes.
Connect this to risks, objectives, change planning and management review of resource adequacy, rather than purchases without improved performance.
Resources may be externally provided under supplier controls, but the organization remains responsible for product and service conformity.
Choose one process, identify its people, infrastructure, environment, measurement and knowledge needs, then compare requirements with availability.
Plan provision, maintenance and follow-up with clear ownership, testing contribution to conformity and results rather than inventory presence alone.
