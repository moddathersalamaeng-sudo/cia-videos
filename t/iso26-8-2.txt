A customer ordered office furniture and agreed on a delivery date.
The items arrived without installation; the customer expected a usable office that same day.
The mismatch began before production and delivery because the meaning of delivery was not clear to both parties.
Clause 8.2 helps clarify requirements, establish capability and review changes before they become customer problems.
Four connected stages: customer communication, determining requirements, review before commitment and control of changes.
We follow the furniture enquiry through to an order the delivery team can act on.
The aim is alignment between promises, requirements and actual capability.
Customer communication covers product and service information, enquiries, contracts, orders and changes, and feedback including complaints.
It also covers customer property and relevant contingency information, including disruptions to provision.
Clarify the offer, feedback route, handling of the customer’s office plan and who communicates a disruption affecting the date.
Determine product and service requirements, including applicable statutory and regulatory requirements and those necessary to the organization.
Ensure the organization can meet the claims made for its products and services.
A claim that installation is included or delivery takes five days must be achievable within the stated conditions.
Marketing claims influence customer expectations and purchases; do not promise beyond capability.
Specify furniture type, dimensions, quantity and finish, and whether transport and installation are included.
Agree on location, timing and applicable post-delivery services such as agreed warranty or support.
For a known intended use, review necessary requirements even if the customer did not state every detail.
For example, item dimensions and the site access route may affect delivery and installation capability.
Review capability to meet requirements before committing to supply.
Review customer requirements, delivery and applicable post-delivery needs, and unstated needs necessary for a known use.
Include organizational requirements, applicable statutory and regulatory requirements, and differences from earlier contract or order terms.
Sales approval alone is insufficient if dimensions, materials or installation capacity have not been appropriately reviewed.
Resolve an offer excluding installation versus a purchase order including it; do not assume the customer accepted the exclusion.
Confirm requirements before acceptance when the customer provides no documented statement of them.

Confirm what is required, rather than merely recording that a call occurred.
