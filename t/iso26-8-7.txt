A supply company prepares an order and finds some units do not meet the agreed size.
Mixing them with conforming orders or unintentionally delivering them would compound the problem.
Clause 8.7 requires identifying and controlling nonconforming outputs to prevent unintended use or delivery.
Make the case known and take action appropriate to the nature and effect of the nonconformity.
Identify the affected output, unmet requirement and known scope of the case.
Prevent unintended use or delivery through controls suitable for the output.
Possible methods include clear identification, appropriate separation or blocking completion in a system.
One physical quarantine area does not automatically suit every product or service.
Use one or more methods: correction; segregation, containment, return or suspension; informing the customer; or authorized acceptance under concession.
Select action according to the nonconformity’s nature and effect on product or service conformity.
Returning or replacing the wrong-size units may suit the applicable requirements and controls.
Informing the customer or receiving a reply does not automatically complete all necessary action.
Acceptance under concession is not a shortcut any employee may choose to speed delivery.
Identify the deviation, the basis for acceptance and the authorized decision-maker under applicable requirements.
A concession does not permit ignoring obligations that cannot be waived.
Include obtained concessions in available documented information about the case.
When nonconforming outputs are corrected, verify conformity to requirements.
Replacing units alone is insufficient; check that the replacements meet requirements.
Then apply relevant release controls under 8.6.
Correction addresses the case; corrective action under 10.2 evaluates the need to eliminate causes to prevent recurrence or occurrence elsewhere.
Make documented evidence available of the nonconformity’s nature, actions taken, concessions obtained and authority deciding the action.
Link evidence to the output to establish what happened, how it was addressed and who decided.
Record formats may vary, but “closed” alone does not establish the evidence.
Use retrievable and verifiable information rather than fields that serve no purpose.
Control also applies to nonconformities found after product delivery or during or after service provision.
For an incorrect service report already sent, identify the affected version and information and act according to its effects.
Connect with communication and applicable post-delivery requirements.
Leaving the organization does not end the need to address a nonconforming output.
Test a real case: identification, control, action, decision-maker and evidence; after correction, retrieve verification of conformity.
