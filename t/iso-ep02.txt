A purchasing officer sees a supplier evaluation template in a course and makes that exact template mandatory.
Is that justified? Not necessarily.
First distinguish the requirement, interpretation, example, tool and evidence.
A requirement states what must be fulfilled. Interpretation explains it without replacing the text.
An example illustrates one approach; a tool helps implement it.
Evidence supports verification that the required work actually happened.
ISO drafting distinguishes requirements, recommendations, permission and possibility or capability.
Shall: requirement. Should: recommendation. May: permission. Can: possibility or capability.
Use a reliable translation and read conditions and exceptions carefully.
Read the whole clause: the required action, its subject, applicable conditions and documented information.
Check definitions, references and related clauses.
Do not omit inconvenient parts or turn an explanatory note or training example into an extra obligation.
In a simplified purchasing example, we select a suitable supplier and monitor performance against clear criteria.
Ask what suitability means for this supply, who decides, and how performance is reviewed.
This illustrates application; it is not the full wording of a clause.
A suitable spreadsheet or digital system may support the process. A training template is not mandatory merely because it is shown.
An applicable contract or approved procedure may add a specific obligation.
Choosing a tool does not mean ignoring requirements or agreed controls.
A blank supplier evaluation form is a tool, not proof that evaluation occurred.
Criteria, results, a decision and its date can provide information for verification.
Completed fields alone are not enough: check relevance, reliability and whether the decision used the data.
Evidence may come from records, observation and interviews whose information can be verified.
Cross-check sources rather than accepting every claim.
Where documented information is required, a verbal explanation does not remove that obligation.
Classify three items: a trainer’s blank template, a completed supplier evaluation, and an applicable text requiring evaluation.
Which is the requirement, the tool, and potential evidence?

The applicable text supplies the requirement; the blank template is a tool; the completed evaluation is potential evidence to examine.
Likewise, a SWOT example in training does not by itself make that format mandatory.
Find the applicable obligation and verify that it is fully met.

In HR, ask whether attendance alone proves ability to perform the task. Match evidence to the intended result.
The requirement sets the obligation; the example explains; the tool helps; evidence supports judgment.
Familiarity alone does not make a template mandatory, and a different format can still provide suitable evidence.
