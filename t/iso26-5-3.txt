A distributor has a shipment ready, but its quantity differs from the customer order and every team waits for someone else.
The warehouse has finished picking, customer service awaits the supervisor, and the supervisor has not heard about the issue.
The gap may be clear responsibilities, authority and communication rather than an extra employee.
A role identifies a function, such as order-preparation supervisor; responsibility identifies the result to be looked after.
Authority defines permitted decisions, such as holding a shipment with a discrepancy for verification.
A job title alone is insufficient: people need their duties, decision limits and escalation route.
Top management ensures responsibilities and authorities for relevant roles are assigned and communicated within the organization.
In practice, link work to a defined role, explain decision limits, and use a realistic situation to check understanding.
Checking understanding is a useful implementation method; sending a job description alone does not show how a quantity discrepancy will be handled.
Assign responsibility and authority for QMS conformity and for reporting its performance and improvement opportunities to top management.
Assign responsibility for processes achieving intended results and for promoting customer focus across the organization.
Maintain QMS integrity continuously, including when changes are planned and implemented, so an improvement in one area does not disrupt another.
Map the order from confirmed customer requirements through picking, verification and authorization to ship.
At each handover define what is transferred, who receives it, acceptance conditions and evidence of completion.
The warehouse receives a confirmed order and returns a picking list; an authorized role checks conformity before shipment release.
Use a table linking work, owner, authority and evidence: customer service confirms the order and records agreed changes.
The warehouse picks and records discrepancies; the authorized supervisor checks release conditions and records authorization.
This table is an optional tool, not a mandatory ISO format. Another responsibility matrix may work if it removes gaps and conflicts.
A discrepancy is found before departure: staff hold shipment within their assigned authority and notify the supervisor.
The supervisor checks and requests correction; if the customer agreement must change, customer service clarifies and confirms it.
An approaching deadline does not override requirements; the authorized person releases after verification, retaining evidence.
Before a supervisor is absent, identify a competent deputy, delegated limits and escalation for decisions outside those limits.
The deputy needs appropriate knowledge and system access; assigning approval while hiding order data is ineffective.
A deputy and clear delegation support continuity; the arrangements depend on the organization and process risks.
Before launching a new order system, review who enters data, verifies it and authorizes shipment.
Test information transfer and user access, explain revised roles, confirm understanding, then monitor the first live orders.
Preserving QMS integrity means avoiding lost checks, inappropriate approval access or broken customer communication during change.
Assign collection and review of picking errors and decision delays, and reporting of QMS performance and improvement opportunities to top management.
Repeated approval waits may call for better delegation or information access rather than blame.
Track the action through suitable measures such as shipment decision time and picking errors, with defined baselines, methods and owners.
Ask staff how they respond to a discrepancy, where their authority ends and whom they contact.
Compare their answer with actual permissions and a recent shipment record to see whether the assigned roles work in practice.
If answers conflict or decisions stall, fix assignments, communication and enabling conditions, then retest rather than collect another signature.
Choose a task with repeated waiting or blame, then define its outcome, owner, decision limits and escalation route.
Test the assignment with the team in a real case, review evidence and improvement results, then extend it to connected processes.
