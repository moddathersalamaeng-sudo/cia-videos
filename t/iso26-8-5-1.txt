At an office-equipment service centre, experienced technicians work from different information and sequences.
One knows the latest order change; the other uses an old version, leading to inconsistent service results.
Skill matters, but it needs controlled operating conditions.
Clause 8.5.1 addresses conditions supporting consistent conformity in production and service provision.
Implement production and service provision under controlled conditions, including the clause elements as applicable.
This does not mean every activity needs identical equipment or inspection forms.
Determine what the product, service and process results need, then make it available and use it.
A procedure on a shelf does not establish that actual execution is controlled.
Make documented information available and use it to define product or service characteristics, activities and intended results.
For servicing, use a clear request, approved scope and suitable instructions instead of individually interpreting the purpose.
Ensure the user accesses and follows valid information.
This connects documented-information control to operational execution.
Make suitable monitoring and measuring resources available and use them.
Select for the intended check: unsuitable equipment or unreliable results do not achieve the purpose.
Determine a suitable service test method with applicable resource controls.
Avoid generic equipment lists or assuming visual checks cover every service characteristic.
Monitor and measure at suitable stages to verify process or output control criteria and product or service acceptance criteria.
Choose points that allow discrepancies to be detected and addressed before it is too late for the work.
Process execution criteria differ from result acceptance criteria; both must be clear.
Handle nonconformity under applicable controls rather than passing it because the deadline is near.
Use suitable infrastructure and an appropriate environment for process operation.
Workspace, equipment arrangement, inspection lighting and information availability may affect service capability.
Determine and maintain suitable conditions according to impact.
The clause does not prescribe one layout or temperature for every organization; suitability to operations and conformity matters.
Appoint competent people, including any required qualifications.
Connect this to demonstrated competence rather than course attendance alone.
People understand requirements, role limits and discrepancy responses, with suitable information and support.
Address competence gaps and control risk during learning rather than assigning influential work without adequate ability.
Where subsequent monitoring or measurement cannot verify the resulting output, validate process capability to achieve planned results and periodically revalidate.
This is a condition tied to process nature, not an automatic requirement for every service or repair activity.
Determine suitable capability evidence, influential conditions and revalidation arrangements as applicable.
Insufficient checking caused by poor practice is not an exemption from control; renaming a simple trial does not establish validation.
Implement actions to prevent human error, suited to the task and potential causes.
Examples include clear information, distinguishing requests, suitable sequencing, alerts or verification at influential points.
Repeating “concentrate more” does not fix a confusing work design.
Review whether the action prevents mix-ups and creates side effects; methods are optional choices according to need.
Implement relevant release, delivery and post-delivery activities.
Finishing a technician’s task is not the whole service: address verification, release authority, delivery and applicable later obligations.
Related clauses provide more detail, while operations need clear arrangements.
The customer receives the agreed outcome, beyond an internal completion status.
Follow one suitable service request through information, resources, competence, checks and discrepancy handling.
Test potential mix-ups, prevention controls, release, delivery and later commitments.
Where process-validation conditions apply, check suitable controls and evidence.
Record gaps, ownership and timing, then evaluate effect; systems, records or linked instructions may be suitable.
Controlled operations combine usable information, suitable resources and environment, competence, timely checks, error prevention and clear service completion.
Review applicability to the process and test actual practice rather than only file existence.
