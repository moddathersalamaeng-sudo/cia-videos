An electronics company finishes assembling an order, and inspection finds it conforming.
Unsuitable handling and storage conditions later leave some units in a different condition from when inspected.
Output quality does not end at the inspection station.
Clause 8.5.4 requires preservation during production and service provision to the extent needed to ensure conformity to requirements.
Preservation means more than packaging.
It can include identification, handling, contamination control, packaging, storage, transmission or transportation, and protection.
Methods depend on the output, requirements and risks.
Maintain conformity through the relevant stages; different activities need not use identical packaging or storage conditions.
Review applicable device-handling requirements and approved design or manufacturing instructions.
Follow the order through assembly, waiting, internal movement, storage and delivery.
At each stage, identify what could compromise conformity and how to prevent or reduce that risk.
Do not invent universal temperature or shelf-life limits; use applicable requirements and product characteristics.
If stacking could damage devices, define suitable stacking and protective packaging.
If parts are sensitive to static electricity, apply appropriate handling and protection according to requirements.
Assign responsibility, provide usable instructions and check compliance and output condition.
These are examples; ISO does not prescribe one packaging type for every device.
For a report service, preservation may include version identification, file protection and intact transmission to the intended recipient.
Transmission method, permissions and version checks follow service requirements.
Connect this with documented information under 7.5 and identification and status under 8.5.2 where applicable.
Different protection methods serve the same goal: preserving output conformity during handling.
For damaged packaging or an incomplete transmitted file, check output condition and determine appropriate action.
Apply nonconforming-output controls under 8.7 where relevant rather than relying on an old inspection result.
Where an issue concerns care of customer property, also consider 8.5.3.
Preservation does not replace release controls or handling of nonconformity.
Choose an actual order and follow its movement between two stages.
Check identification, handling, packaging, storage or transmission, and preserved condition; seek suitable evidence and review gaps, ownership, action and results.
A checklist or handling-damage indicator may help, but this clause does not prescribe one form or target percentage.
Preserve conformity during production and service provision to the extent necessary.
Choose methods based on requirements and risks and review them in the actual workflow.
