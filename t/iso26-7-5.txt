At an assembly plant, an instruction was revised and approved, but an old paper copy remained at the workstation and was followed.
The correct file existed, yet the product was nonconforming because control of information in use was incomplete.
Clause 7.5 addresses creation, updating and control so documented information is suitable, available when needed and protected against improper use.
Include documented information required by the standard and information the organization determines necessary for QMS effectiveness.
The extent varies with size, activities, process complexity and interactions, and people’s competence; do not copy a large system without need.
Start with process needs and the risk of missing information or evidence; clause 7.5 does not itself mandate a named quality manual or a procedure for every activity.
An assembly instruction guides execution; an inspection record evidences what occurred and the result. Both are documented information, with controls suited to purpose.
Information may be paper, electronic, a drawing, image or system data, rather than text documents alone.
When instructions change, direct use to valid information; do not rewrite a historical result record to imply work happened differently.
When creating or updating, ensure suitable identification and description, such as a title, date, author or reference as appropriate.
Choose a suitable format and medium, including language, graphics or software version where relevant to use.
Review and approve for suitability and adequacy: information must meet the work’s needs, beyond a signature on an incomplete page.
Control information so it is available and suitable where and when needed, and adequately protected from confidentiality loss, improper use or loss of integrity.
The operator accesses the approved workstation instruction, while editing follows defined authority; viewing need not imply editing permission.
Protection should not obstruct needed work, and availability does not mean everyone may alter every record.
Address distribution, access, retrieval and use as applicable; sending a file alone does not control proliferating copies.
Define the approved working reference, how users access it and how related order or batch inspection records can be retrieved.
Test actual access to the correct instruction and retrieval of the needed record, rather than relying only on a file list.
Storage and preservation include legibility: an unreadable scan or inaccessible file cannot serve its purpose when needed.
Choose suitable protection for paper and files, potentially including backups and restoration checks according to the system and risks.
Backup is one possible method, not prescribed software; preserve usable content for the required period.
Control the change end to end: review, identify and approve the instruction, then update the actual point of use and inform affected people.
Check paper and local copies; if obsolete information is retained, control it to prevent unintended use.
A version number alone does not help if the old instruction still appears current; verify what the operator actually sees at the workstation.
Determine retention and disposition according to information type, organizational needs and applicable customer, legal and contractual requirements.
Clause 7.5 does not prescribe one retention period for every record; loss of daily use does not mean evidence has no further value.
Before disposal, verify that retention needs and obligations are satisfied, then act under suitable controls protecting confidentiality and the decision.
Identify and control external-origin documented information that the organization determines necessary for QMS planning and operation.
Examples include customer drawings, material specifications or equipment manuals; define the relevant reference, status and handling of significant updates.
An external source does not remove responsibility for controlled use; not every downloaded file automatically becomes a necessary QMS reference.
Protect documented evidence of conformity from unintended alteration; an inspection record is not an unrestricted space to rewrite results.
If a recording error occurs, correct it under suitable controls with an understandable correction trail, rather than hiding results or inventing an inspection.
Editing permissions and change tracking are possible controls, supporting honesty and reliable conformity evidence.
Choose one instruction and one record; test identification, review, permissions, retrieval, old copies and retention, then address gaps affecting execution or evidence.
