A training centre promises a suitable programme and engages an external trainer.
During delivery, content fails to match the agreed level; the centre attributes the problem to the trainer.
The customer agreed a result with the centre; external delivery does not remove organizational accountability.
Clause 8.4 supports selecting, controlling and monitoring external provision according to conformity impact.
Determine controls when external provision is incorporated into your offering, delivered directly to your customer on your behalf, or performs a process or part of it.
Our example includes printed training materials, an external trainer and outsourced platform operation.
The arrangements differ, but external provision must conform to requirements; control goes beyond a purchase invoice.
Define and apply evaluation and selection criteria based on capability to provide conforming processes, products or services.
Trainer criteria may include relevant experience, explaining ability, a suitable work sample and needed competence or qualifications.
Printer criteria may instead cover legibility, content accuracy and delivery capability.
These are context-specific examples, not one prescribed checklist for every provider.
After selection, monitor performance and re-evaluate using suitable criteria; initial approval is not permanent clearance.
Review content conformity, timeliness and response to findings before deciding on continuation, increased controls or replacement.
Make documented evidence available for evaluation, selection, monitoring, re-evaluation and necessary actions arising from evaluations.
ISO does not prescribe a universal score form or annual re-evaluation for all cases.
Ensure external provision does not adversely affect consistent delivery of conforming products or services.
Consider impact on customer and applicable statutory and regulatory requirements, and the effectiveness of the provider’s own controls.
A specialist programme may need content review and an appropriate trainer trial, while a simple purchase needs different controls.
Avoid identical control for every provider or exemption based only on reputation.
Define controls on the provider and on resulting outputs; externally provided processes remain within QMS control.
Trainer competence review alone does not prove suitable content, and one print sample does not automatically prove every copy.
Determine necessary verification or other activities to establish conformity.
Choose methods and extent according to impact and existing controls, rather than universal 100% inspection.
Ensure requirements are adequate before communicating them to the external provider.
“An excellent course” does not define audience level, intended results, content scope, duration or delivery method.
Communicate what enables the trainer to deliver and the centre to judge conformity.
Keep requirements consistent with customer agreement and needed capability, rather than blaming the provider for unstated expectations.
Communicate requirements, as appropriate, for provision and approval of products or services, methods, processes, equipment and release.
Communicate competence requirements, including needed qualifications.
For example, define suitable content review before delivery and who approves significant changes.
These controls suit the example; identical prior approvals are not mandatory for all providers.
Also communicate provider interactions with the organization and, where applicable, customers and other relevant parties, and applied performance controls and monitoring.
Communicate intended verification or validation activities by the organization or its customer at provider premises or another location, as appropriate.
For the trainer, clarify coordination, customer contact and handling of changes and feedback.
Planned observation, trials or reviews should not be an unexpected requirement introduced after delivery begins.
If content is unsuitable before delivery, determine an appropriate response and apply relevant nonconformity controls.
A response may involve correction and re-verification or another authorized arrangement, considering effects on commitments.
Analyze the cause: provider capability, unclear centre requirements or an uncommunicated change.
Use the result in monitoring, re-evaluation and cause-focused action where needed.
Connect requirements, evaluation and selection criteria, approval decisions, agreed controls, performance results and actions.
Test one programme: does the trainer know the requirements, can the centre judge content, and are change and failure responses clear?
Information may be in a system or linked records; control and evidence matter more than an approved list or certificate alone.
For an influential provider, connect requirements to capability, controls to impact and monitoring to actual results.
Purchasing, operations and quality then work from a shared basis.
