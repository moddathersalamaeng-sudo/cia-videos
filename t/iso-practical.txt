A quality management system helps the organization understand needs, provide capability, act, check and learn.

Focus on actions, results and evidence: a record shows what happened, but cannot perform the work.
Determine relevant internal and external issues, including whether climate change is relevant, and monitor and review that information.
Determine relevant interested parties and requirements, including which will be addressed through the QMS.
Define scope and covered products and services, and determine and manage processes, interactions, criteria, resources and responsibilities.
At the service centre, intake must pass fault information and requirements to the technician; drawing a map alone does not repair a missing handover.
Top management is accountable for QMS effectiveness, integration, resources, quality culture, ethical behaviour and improvement.
Maintain customer focus, applicable requirements and relevant conformity and satisfaction risks and opportunities.
Establish a suitable quality policy that is understood and applied, and assign and communicate responsibilities and authorities.
Know who can authorize commitments or stop a nonconforming release; a signature alone does not establish leadership.
Determine, analyse and evaluate risks and opportunities; plan suitable actions, integration into processes and effectiveness evaluation.
Set measurable quality objectives and determine actions, resources, ownership, timing and evaluation.
Plan system changes, considering purpose, consequences, resources, information, responsibilities, communication and effectiveness and results reviews.
Before expanding service, review technician capacity and parts availability; a risk table alone does not implement action.
Provide suitable people, infrastructure, working environment and monitoring and measuring resources, and maintain fitness for purpose.
Where measurement traceability is required or essential, apply calibration or verification, identification and protection requirements, and address invalid equipment effects.
Determine necessary knowledge; retain, apply and share it, and address updating needs.
An available tool is not enough: it must suit the work, produce dependable results and be supported by accessible knowledge.
Determine and ensure competence, take applicable actions and evaluate their effectiveness, with appropriate competence evidence.
Ensure required awareness, including policy, relevant objectives, contribution, consequences, quality culture and ethics; define relevant communications.
Provide required and necessary documented information, and appropriately review, approve, make available, protect, change and retain it.
Observe competent use of the current method; attendance and a stored file alone do not prove capability or implementation.
Plan and control operation, process and acceptance criteria, resources, changes and external provision.
Communicate, determine applicable requirements, review ability before commitment, and update information and notify relevant parties about changes.
Where design and development applies, control planning, inputs, reviews, verification, validation, outputs and changes.
Pass agreed requirements to the technician; a service activity does not automatically make design and development inapplicable.
Define and apply supplier evaluation, selection, performance-monitoring and re-evaluation criteria, with required evidence and necessary actions.
Select controls considering potential impact and supplier-control effectiveness, and determine needed verification.
Ensure requirements are adequate before communication, including relevant output, approval, competence, interaction, monitoring and verification needs.
A familiar supplier does not establish part conformity; apply suitable controls rather than relying only on a supplier list.
Provide controlled service with competent people, suitable information, resources and checks, including process validation where later verification is not possible.
Manage identification, status and required traceability; protect customer property, preserve outputs and control changes and post-delivery requirements.
Release after satisfactory completion of planned arrangements, except under required approval as applicable, with conformity and authorization evidence.
Identify and control nonconforming outputs, take appropriate action and verify conformity after correction; a completed-repair signature cannot replace checking.
Define what, methods and timing for monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation, with evidence of results.
Monitor satisfaction and evaluate required conformity, satisfaction, QMS, planning, risk and opportunity actions, provider performance and improvement needs.
Repair time is one input; recurring faults and satisfaction information add to the picture.
A green indicator alone does not establish that service achieved its purpose.
Audit at planned intervals with a suitable programme, defined objectives, criteria and scope, impartiality, reporting, appropriate action and evidence.
Top management reviews continuing suitability, adequacy, effectiveness and strategic alignment at planned intervals.
Use all required inputs and produce improvement, QMS-change and resource decisions, with documented review results.
Discuss recurring faults and capacity issues to reach supported decisions; reports or minutes alone do not fulfil the purpose.
Continually improve QMS suitability, adequacy and effectiveness; determine and address opportunities using information and management-review results.
For nonconformity, react, evaluate causes and similar cases, implement needed action, review effectiveness and update risks, opportunities and the QMS if necessary, with required evidence.
Correcting one device addresses that case; preventing recurrence needs cause-appropriate action and an effectiveness check.
