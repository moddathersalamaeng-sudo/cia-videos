HR wants to design a service for welcoming new employees.
An organized start is the goal, but “excellent onboarding” alone does not define how the service works.
Design and development turn needs into defined product or service characteristics that can be implemented, reviewed and tested.
This applies to service design as well as engineering drawings where design and development are applicable to the organization’s QMS.
Establish, implement and maintain an appropriate design and development process to support subsequent product or service provision.
Stages and controls depend on nature, complexity and failure impact; development may involve repeated cycles.
A form or end-of-project signature alone does not establish a functioning process.
Transform requirements into a suitable deliverable with necessary controls and evidence.
Plan considering the nature, duration and complexity of activities, required stages and suitable design reviews.
Consider verification and validation activities, responsibilities, authorities and internal and external resources.
Also consider control of interfaces between people involved.
For onboarding, define HR, manager and IT roles and decision authority so no essential part is overlooked.
Planning also considers involvement of customers and relevant parties, subsequent provision requirements and their expected level of control.
Determine documented information needed to evidence that design and development requirements are met.
Involve service users and review the needs of people and resources that will deliver it.
An attractive experience is insufficient if the delivery team cannot operate it.
Determine essential requirements for the product or service type, considering function, performance and information from previous similar designs.
Consider statutory and regulatory requirements, committed standards or codes of practice, and potential consequences of failure.
For onboarding, define what employees need to start work, required information and permissions, and applicable organizational obligations.
These are design inputs, rather than assumptions chosen by the designer alone.
Inputs must be complete, unambiguous and adequate; resolve conflicts and make documented evidence available.
Do not pass conflicting requests for unrestricted access and role-based permission controls into implementation.
Clarify the real need and design an arrangement meeting it within the controls.
Inputs may evolve during development; control updates rather than leaving conflicting assumptions.
Define intended results and apply suitable design controls.
Review evaluates whether design results can meet requirements and identifies problems needing action.
Verification checks that outputs meet input requirements.
Validation checks that the resulting product or service meets the specified application or intended use.
The purposes differ; activities may be separate or combined as appropriate.
Review the onboarding flow with HR, the manager and IT.
A problem emerges: account preparation starts after the date tools were promised to be ready.
Review exposes a capability gap; adjust sequencing or responsibilities through a clear decision.
Meeting attendance does not prove resolution: follow the action and its effect on the design.
For verification, take a defined input, such as clear approval roles for permissions, and compare it with the design output.
Does the workflow or system identify the approver, request limits and rejection response? Document review or system testing may be suitable.
Evidence of output-to-input conformity alone does not establish suitability of the employee’s actual experience for intended use.
Validate the service under conditions representing intended use, checking that employees and managers can complete necessary tasks without significant gaps.
Use suitable representative cases within privacy and operational controls, rather than an uncontrolled launch awaiting complaints.
An unclear next step may reveal an experience problem even when all fields exist.
Address findings and document control activities, results and necessary actions.
Ensure outputs meet input requirements and are adequate for subsequent product or service provision processes.
Include or reference appropriate monitoring and measuring requirements and acceptance criteria, plus essential characteristics and information for safe, proper intended provision.
Outputs may include the service flow, roles, forms or system requirements and acceptance criteria.
Make documented output evidence available; an attractive presentation cannot replace implementation detail.
Determine, review and control changes during or after development as needed to prevent adverse conformity effects.
When removing an approval step, assess permissions, roles, tests and related processes; apply further verification or validation according to impact.
Make evidence available for changes, review results, authorization and actions preventing adverse effects.
A small interface change may alter an important service control.
Link a development need to clear inputs, implementable outputs, and review, verification and validation according to purpose.
Check that problems are addressed, changes controlled and evidence available.
