A customer gives an engineering office project drawings; an external provider lends it measuring equipment.
Neither belongs to the office, but both are under its control or being used.
Sharing drawings with an unauthorized recipient or harmful equipment storage affects someone else’s property.
Clause 8.5.3 requires care while property is under organizational control or being used.
Property means more than equipment and materials.
It can include components, tools, premises, intellectual property and personal data.
Drawings are valuable information; the equipment is a physical asset.
Candidate data in human resources is another example.
Identify the property of others held by your organization and suitable care for its nature and use.
Different property may need different methods.
Identify, verify, protect and safeguard property.
Record the owner and version of drawings; check that the intended file opens and suits the planned use.
For the equipment, identify it, its condition and accessories, and verify suitability for the work.
These are possible methods selected to suit the risks.
An intake record alone is insufficient if subsequent use is uncontrolled.
For drawings, control access and sharing and prevent use of an unapproved version in execution.
For equipment, determine who uses it, where it is kept and how it is handled appropriately.
If equipment affects measurement validity, connect its use to monitoring and measuring resources under 7.1.5.
Where drawings are documented information used in the system, apply relevant controls under 7.5.
During use, staff find equipment damage or a customer file unsuitable for its intended purpose.
Do not conceal the problem or continue inappropriate use.
Report it to the customer or external provider who owns the property, and make documented evidence of what occurred available.
Identify the affected property, known facts and actions taken.
Formats may vary; reporting and documented evidence are required.
Apply the approach to the borrowed equipment.
On receipt, know its identity, owner and condition.
Before use, verify suitability.
Control its use and storage during the work.
On finding damage, prevent inappropriate use, report it to the owner and document what happened.
Assess whether earlier work may be affected, and apply nonconforming-output controls under 8.7 where relevant.
Reporting alone does not establish that the effects have been resolved.
Choose an actual customer file or borrowed tool.
Can you identify its owner and condition, verify suitability, know authorized users and demonstrate protection during work and reporting evidence after an incident?
This distinguishes written procedures from actual care; a property register is a possible method, not one mandatory ISO form.
