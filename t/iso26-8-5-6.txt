A printing company changes the sequence of an order’s production stages to meet delivery timing.
The change seems small, but some outputs develop a problem absent from the previous method.
The issue is changing production without suitable review and control, rather than change itself.
Clause 8.5.6 requires reviewing and controlling production or service changes to the extent needed to ensure continuing conformity.
Identify the step whose sequence will change, affected orders and the reason.
Review requirements, acceptance criteria, resources, instructions and people who may be affected.
Success of the previous method does not establish success of the new one.
Scale review to the change and its effects rather than requiring the same study for every adjustment.
Review whether the proposed sequence will preserve planned results and what actions are needed before or during implementation.
Depending on the change, actions may include a limited trial, suitable checks, instruction updates or resource preparation.
A trial is a possible method, not one prescribed step for every change.
Following review, the authorized person approves the change under the organization’s system; a proposal should not automatically become execution.
Make documented evidence available of review results, people authorizing the change and any necessary actions arising from review.
Link the print-order change with its review result, authorizer and resulting actions.
A change form or electronic record may help; one format is not prescribed for everyone.
A signature alone does not cover review results or necessary actions.
Assume review identifies a potential effect from changing stage sequence.
Define suitable verification, update execution instructions, assign responsibility and authorize the change.
After implementation, verify continuing conformity under applicable controls and avoid mixing old and new instructions.
Apply 8.7 where outputs are nonconforming; authorizing a change does not authorize delivering a defect.
Clause 6.3 addresses planning changes to the quality management system.
Clause 8.5.6 focuses on changes to production and service provision.
If design and development are affected, also apply relevant design-change controls under 8.3.6.
Control instructions or records used in the system under documented-information requirements in 7.5.
Choose a recent production or service change.
Retrieve its review result, authorizer and necessary resulting actions, then follow implementation and effects on conformity.
If a change occurred without adequate control, assess and address it instead of backdating approval as if prior review had taken place.
A checklist or change register can help identify and address gaps.
Controlled change involves suitable review, authorization, necessary actions and available evidence.
The purpose is continuing conformity rather than simply completing a form.
